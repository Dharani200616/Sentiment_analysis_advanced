🎯 Detailed Confusion Matrix Analysis
📊 Understanding Matrix Subdivisions

The Confusion Matrix divides our model's test predictions into four key quadrants:

True Positives (TP) Correct positive reviews.
True Negatives (TN) Correct negative reviews.
False Positives (FP) Negative reviews predicted as Positive.
False Negatives (FN) Positive reviews predicted as Negative.
🔍 Diagnostic Insights

If your model contains high numbers of False Positives, it is overly optimistic, misclassifying complaints as praises. If it exhibits high False Negatives, it is missing customer appreciation. Balanced diagonals show a highly stable classifier.

💡 How it Works

The TF-IDF Vectorizer maps terms to numerical vectors. The classifier then assigns positive or negative multiplier coefficients to each word. When a new review is classified, the sum of product weights determines the final sentiment confidence.

📈 Receiver Operating Characteristic & Sensitivity
🎯 Area Under Curve (AUC)

The **ROC AUC** (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic) represents the probability that our NLP classifier ranks a random positive feedback higher than a random negative one.

* **0.90 - 1.00**: Outstanding separation. * **0.70 - 0.90**: Good/Excellent classifier. * **0.50**: Completely random classification.

🎯 Precision-Recall Utility

While ROC curves are ideal for balanced datasets, a **Precision-Recall Curve** (PR Curve) is much more robust for highly skewed datasets (e.g. 95% positive customer reviews, 5% complaints) since it focuses specifically on the minority class performance.
